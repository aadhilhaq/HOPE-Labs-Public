Prepare

A short CPU job that takes an RCSB entry, a complex file or an AlphaFold model from the file as it came to a structure an engine can build on: cleaned, modeled, protonated, minimized and split into receptor and partner. Every step says what it did.

When it runs

The Prepare step appears for the three sources that arrive whole. You need not press anything: Submit queues Prepare as the first job of the run's graph whenever the structure has not been prepared, the build waits for it, and the job writes the run's files again from the prepared structure before the build starts.

Press Prepare to run it on its own first and look at the result. The job goes to the short partition (8 CPUs, 16 GB, an hour at most) and usually takes 20 to 75 seconds once it starts, or a few minutes when it builds loops (7A3R's five took five); the note under the button follows it from queued to running to done. The viewer then shows the prepared receptor and partner, and the checks list each step Prepare logged, a ▲ beside any that was not done, failed or was skipped. A step whose program is missing is skipped and says so.

The other sources do not go through Prepare: the import does what it needs (see Receptor and ligand files), and on the Amber-topology engines the build job assigns the receptor's protonation states with PROPKA at the run's pH.

What Prepare does, in order

  1. Clean, as above. The crystal waters kept leave the structure until the split, modified amino acids become their parent, and the stage stops on a modified residue whose charge is not its parent's, or when no protein or nucleic-acid chain is left.
  2. Trim, for a predicted model only: the terminal residues under the pLDDT threshold. This comes before modeling, so a trimmed tail is never rebuilt.
  3. Model with PDBFixer: non-standard residues replaced by standard ones, missing heavy atoms added, and the gaps inside a chain up to the limit built, then relaxed: see Missing residues. A gap in the numbering whose flanking residues are already bonded is a skipped number, not a hole, and nothing is built there.
  4. Protonate at the pH. Clean's hetero groups then leave the structure, and the ions the builds have parameters for are carried on at their own coordinates. Each protein gap left open is capped, an NME on the residue before it and an ACE on the residue after it; a nucleic-acid break is ended so that both pieces have their own termini.
  5. Minimize, with the atoms that were in the file held near where they were.
  6. Split the structure into the receptor and the partner, with the carried ions and the kept waters in files of their own, and check where the partner sits against the prepared receptor.

Missing residues

Prepare finds missing residues from the sequence the file records (its SEQRES), and builds them as below.

  • Only a gap inside a chain is built, and only up to Model loops up to residues, 12 at most. A chain's missing ends are never built: a tail is held at one end only, and built it lies across the solvent as a straight strand (7A3R's 38-residue tail spanned 177 Å).
  • PDBFixer builds the loop from a fixed seed, and the relaxation below is seeded and run on one thread per attempt, so the same structure gives the same loop every time.
  • The loop is then relaxed before protonation. The built residues move freely. The two measured residues either side, its stems, may give a little on a spring. Every other measured atom is held where it was measured. While it relaxes, every peptide bond in and beside the loop is held trans, and every built residue and stem of the L hand; a built residue PDBFixer left of the D hand is set right first.
  • Every peptide bond in and beside the loop is then checked: the C-N bond between 1.30 and 1.37 Å, the CA-C-N angle between 105 and 130°, the C-N-CA angle between 110 and 135°, CA to CA between 3.70 and 3.95 Å, and the twist within 30° of trans (a cis bond passes only before a proline). Every built residue must be of the L hand, and no heavy atom of one may come within 2.5 Å of another more than three bonds away. Up to five relaxations are tried, each with looser stems than the one before.
  • If no relaxation makes the loops fit, they are built again from the next of three seeds. If none fits, Prepare stops and says so: lower Model loops up to, or set it to 0, to prepare the structure as measured, its gaps capped.
  • In the minimization at the end, the built residues are free and the measured ones are held; the built loops keep their bonds trans and their residues L, and are checked once more as they are written.

A built residue takes the number PDBFixer gives it, which is that of the missing residue (A:191 to A:194 on 7A3R). Where that number belongs to a residue already there, as inside a run of insertion codes, the built residue takes the next free insertion code instead, so no built residue takes the id of a measured one. The report lists what was built, from which seed, how far the stems moved, and every gap left open with the reason.

Crystal waters

By default every crystal water goes. keep near the partner keeps those whose oxygen is within 5 Å of a heavy atom of the partner or ligand: the first shell, and the waters bridging it to the receptor. With no partner, none is near one and none is kept. keep all keeps every one.

The waters kept go into the build after the solute and its ions, as the run's own water model (OPC waters in an OPC run, for instance), on every engine. The solvation places no water over them, and the analysis and MM-GBSA take them as solvent. A kept water that an atom Prepare modeled or moved now overlaps, within 2.2 Å of its oxygen, is left out, and the report names it. A bilayer is built without crystal waters: see Membranes.

Metal and other ions

The ions Clean keeps (the metals, the cations a nucleic acid binds, and the monovalent ions when the additives are kept) go into the build as free ions where the structure has them. These are the ones the builds have parameters for:

Each carries the 12-6 parameters of the ion set that goes with the run's water model; on Desmond, OPLS's. The ions a DNA or RNA binds, within 3.5 Å of it, such as the K+ of a G-quadruplex, stay when the additives and their ions are dropped. They go with the metals when Keep cofactors is off, and a name under Also drop goes whatever binds it.

MM-GBSA counts each ion with the side whose residues bind it: the ions that bind the partner are counted with the partner, the rest with the receptor. Beside a small molecule they are all counted with the receptor. The analysis keeps every carried ion with the solute; the box's own salt is stripped with the water.

A metal site is not held by a free ion. A zinc, iron, copper, cobalt, nickel, manganese, cadmium or mercury ion in its site goes in as a charge and a sphere with no bond to the residues around it, and a cysteine that binds it is neutral, not a thiolate. Nothing holds it in place, and it can leave its site during the equilibration. A bonded model of the site (ZAFF, MCPB.py) is not built here: the report names the residues around each one, so check the site in the trajectory.

A modified amino acid whose charge is not its parent's is never mapped: a phosphate on a serine, threonine, tyrosine or histidine (SEP, TPO, PTR, NEP), a sulfate on a tyrosine (TYS), a carboxylate on a lysine or a glutamate (KCX, CGU), or the acetyl that takes a lysine's charge (ALY). As the parent the run would be another molecule with another charge, and no force field here has parameters for the residue itself, so HOPE-MD does not simulate its chain. The import names it, the chain's field turns red, and Prepare stops if it reaches one. A modified nucleotide is refused the same way. Leave the chain out of the receptor and partner chains, or start from a structure without the modification.

Chain breaks and stretched bonds

On every route, a chain is ended where residues are missing, so the build makes no bond across the gap. Two consecutive residues are a break when the link between them (a protein's C to N, a nucleic acid's O3′ to P) is longer than a bond and either a residue is missing between them (a number, or an insertion code of the same number: 60A to 60C) or they are more than 4 Å apart. The checks and the report list the breaks, such as A:228-235. A loop Prepare models is not a break, and nor is a skipped number or an insertion code between two residues that are bonded.

Prepare caps the two ends of a protein gap. The residue before the gap gets an N-methylamide (NME) and the residue after it an acetyl (ACE), so each end is a neutral amide, as it is in the intact protein. Left as termini, the ends of an open gap would be an NH3+ and a COO− the protein does not have, possibly beside the binding site. The chain's own termini stay charged. Each cap is built on the backbone of its residue, with standard bond lengths and a trans peptide bond, and the minimization leaves it free to settle. The prepared file writes the caps as ATOM records under the names the Amber force fields and OpenMM use: C, O, CH3 and H1 to H3 for an ACE; N, H, C and H1 to H3 for an NME, whose methyl carbon is called C. With Cap open gaps off, and at a nucleic-acid break, each piece ends in charged termini: an OXT on the piece before the gap and an NH3+ on the piece after it.

The NME takes the number one past the residue before the gap, and the ACE the number one short of the residue after it, so A:228-235 is capped by NME A:229 and ACE A:234. These are the numbers of the missing residues whose backbone atoms the caps stand in for. No residue of the structure is renumbered and no cap takes a number a residue already has, so a hotspot list still names the residues it named before. Where a single residue is missing, the ACE shares the NME's number with an insertion code (A:229A). A gap with no number free beside it takes insertion codes of the number before it. Inside a run of insertion codes these are the ids of the missing residues: thrombin's H:149A-149E is capped by NME H:149B and ACE H:149D. Between two residues numbered in sequence they are the first free ones: A:3-4 is capped by NME A:3A and ACE A:3B. The build numbers every residue in file order, so an insertion code is as good as a number there. The ACE is placed clear of its neighbors: it is turned about its residue's N-CA bond until each of its heavy atoms is 2.8 Å or more from every heavy atom but that residue's N and CA.

A structure that arrives capped, from an earlier preparation, keeps its caps where they are. pdb2pqr has no definition for ACE or NME, so the structure is protonated without them and they go back on afterwards; no gap is capped twice.

A link longer than a bond with no residue missing is a bond the deposited model stretched. It stays a bond, the minimization closes it, and the report names it with its length, such as A:64-65 (C–N 3.14 Å). Look at these in the viewer: a residue lost from a renumbered file can leave its neighbors about this far apart. Desmond builds bonds by geometry, so from an import of files, which Prepare does not touch, it leaves a stretched link open where the Amber-topology engines build it as a bond.

Insertion codes and skipped numbers do not break a chain in the build. pdb4amber, which the Amber, OpenMM, GROMACS and NAMD builds read the structure with, joins a residue to the next only when their numbers follow each other. A chain numbered with insertion codes (thrombin's H:60A to H:60I, an antibody's H:82A) or with numbers its scheme skips would come apart at each one, with charged termini on both sides. The build therefore ends the real breaks with TER first, on the structure's own numbers, and then gives pdb4amber each file with its residues numbered 1, 2, 3 … in file order, which is how tleap numbers the topology anyway. Each residue's own chain, number and insertion code are kept in build/receptor_numbering.json, and in build/ligand_numbering.json for a partner made of chains. The report and the Runs screen name a residue of the topology by them: a contact lost on thrombin reads L:14C@OE2 – H:202@NZ rather than :284@OE2_:212@NZ, and the RMSF tile names the residue that moves most.

B-factors and pLDDT

A predicted model writes its confidence, pLDDT, where an experiment writes B-factors. They are read as pLDDT only for a predicted model:

  • an AlphaFold result, always;
  • a file that says it is a model: ModelCIF declaring a local pLDDT metric, an AlphaFold DB title, or a file named as AlphaFold and ColabFold name theirs;
  • a file that names no source, when every residue carries one value on all its atoms, as AlphaFold writes pLDDT, where refined B-factors differ from atom to atom.

An RCSB entry is never read so, nor is a file that declares an experimental method, and the column has to look like pLDDT in the first place (no Cα above 100, a mean above 30, some above 90). Only then are the termini trimmed: from each end of each chain, the residues under the threshold, stopping at the first that passes. Low-confidence stretches inside a chain are kept. The report says how the B-factors were read, and why.

Protonation

The pH is on the Structure card: 7.4 by default, anything from 0 to 14. Prepare assigns the protonation states for it in one of three ways:

ChoiceWhat it does
propka at the pH (pdb2pqr)The default. PROPKA decides each titratable residue's state at the pH, and the residues are named as the Amber force fields name them (HID, HIE, HIP, ASH, GLH, LYN, CYX). The checks list how many of each. A cysteine PROPKA makes a thiolate is set back to a neutral cysteine.
PDBFixer rulesPDBFixer's own rules at the pH.
noneNothing is assigned here, and the build's defaults apply.

Should pdb2pqr fail, the report says so and the defaults apply. A ligand bound in the structure is set for pH 7.4 at import, whatever this pH is: see A ligand bound in the structure.

Minimization

A short minimization with OpenMM, in vacuum, to relieve the clashes the modeling made. The heavy atoms of every residue that was in the file are held near their place at 50 kcal/mol/Å²; the residues Prepare modeled are free to find theirs. The report gives how far the held atoms moved (their RMSD) and how far the modeled ones went.

Clashes are counted before the modeling and after the minimization: pairs of heavy atoms closer than 2.2 Å that are not in the same or neighboring residues, disulfides and metal sites aside. The checks show the two counts, before and after.

The split

The receptor chains and the partner chains are written apart, the ions that bind the partner first among the carried ions so the partner's residue range takes them, and the kept waters last. Then the placement check of Sources is made on the prepared structure. A ligand bound in the structure and run as the partner sat out the minimization, so atoms Prepare modeled or moved can overlap it: the check says so in those words, and you should look at the pocket before you submit.

A disulfide across the split

A disulfide can join a receptor chain to a partner chain: thrombin's light chain to its heavy chain, insulin's A chain to its B chain, an antibody's light chain to its heavy chain. The split finds each one, and the checks strip says covalently linked and names the pair. The build bonds it, so the run is one molecule, as the protein is:

  • On Amber, OpenMM, GROMACS and NAMD, both cysteines are named CYX and tleap bonds them by their places in the topology; in a bilayer, OpenMM's Modeller bonds them. The build checks that the bond it makes is the pair the structure has, and stops with the reason if not.
  • On Desmond, every disulfide goes to Maestro as an SSBOND record, which is how its reader learns of a bond between two sulfurs.

MM-GBSA is not run on such a run: see When the two sides are bonded. To score an interface, split the complex where no bond crosses.
