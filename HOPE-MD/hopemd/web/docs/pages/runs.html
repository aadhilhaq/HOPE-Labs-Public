The Runs screen

Every run in the runs folder with its state, a row for every job of the one you open, its results beside its trajectory, and the buttons that act on it.

The list of runs

The column on the left lists the runs in the folder named above it, newest first, each with a dot for its state, its name, its engine and when its folder last changed. Rescan reads the folder again, and Another folder opens another for the session: see The runs folder. Click a run to open it; the page reads it again every 30 seconds while it is open.

Progress

Under the list, Progress has a row for every job of the open run's graph, and for every replicate of the jobs that run one per replicate, in the order the run goes through them: Prepare, the ligand's parameters, the build, each replicate's equilibration and production segments, the analysis, each replicate's MM-GBSA, then tidy. Each row says its state (queued, running, done, failed, timed out, canceled, out of memory) with a bar under it; a production row gives the nanoseconds done of its segment, and its speed while it runs. Where Slurm's accounting has nothing, the state is read from what the run folder holds.

Click a row to follow that job's log: its last lines appear under the buttons, with the job's state, node and time, and they refresh every 5 seconds while it runs. Click the row again to stop following it.

Results and the trajectory

The run's name heads the screen, with its engine, force field and water, its length and replicates, and its atoms once built. Under it:

  • The tiles, once the analysis job has run: the RMSDs, the buried surface, the RMSF, the contacts kept, the area per lipid in a bilayer, and an AlphaFold model's ipTM. See Analysis and MM-GBSA.
  • The trajectory of a replicate, with the same bar as the Setup viewer: color, draw, surfaces, what to show, one chain drawn differently, reset, spin, save image and add to report, which puts the frame as it is drawn into the run's report (Adding a view). A surface is computed by MSMS on the frame shown when it is drawn, and stays as the trajectory plays. Metal ions are drawn as spheres, each in its own color, and named in the legend over the viewer; a run that carries a metal keeps the waters nearest its sites, chosen again at every frame, and Water shows or hides them. The bulk of the box is not kept. Under it, a scrubber with play, the frame and its time, the replicate, and the view: the strided one, every 20th saved frame over the whole run, or a dense window.
  • ±5 ns here cuts a dense window around the frame shown, at close to the save interval and up to 100 frames: a cpptraj job of a few seconds on the short partition. It joins the view list when it is ready.
  • Water, under Show, shows or hides the waters at the metal sites in a run that carries a metal. In any other run it draws the 30 waters nearest the partner (or the receptor, in a run with none) in each frame of the strided view: a cpptraj job of a minute or less on the short partition, queued by the first press, after which the button turns them on and off. It is a picture of the shell, not of any one molecule: cpptraj picks the nearest 30 again in every frame, so a water is not followed from one frame to the next, and the rest of the box is left out. A dense window is cut without water.
  • MM-GBSA over frames: each replicate's frames, its mean binding energy in kcal/mol and the standard error, filled when the MM-GBSA job ends.
  • Jobs: what ran where, for how long: see The timing record.

Start again from a stage

Under Run it again, the list beside Start again holds the stages of the run's graph, and opens on the last production segment. Pressing it submits that stage and everything that depends on it again, with the dependencies rebuilt: the stages before it keep their jobs, and the new jobs replace the old in the rows and the Jobs table, the old ones kept in the run's record.

This is the answer to a segment that hit its walltime, a node that failed, or a stage that failed for a reason since put right. A production segment that was stopped continues from its last restart point rather than its start, and the frames it wrote past that point are cut first, so none repeats: see Production in segments.

Tidy

Scratch counts files as well as bytes, and a finished run leaves 100 to 250 of them. Tidy packs the ones nothing reads any more (stage logs, intermediate restarts and checkpoints, the build's scratch files) into one archive in the run's archive/ folder, so the run holds tens of files instead of hundreds. The trajectories, the restart points a resume needs, the analysis, the report and the media stay as they are.

It is the last job of every run's graph unless pack small files when done was unticked. It runs once the production and the analysis have succeeded and MM-GBSA has ended, whether or not MM-GBSA succeeded. Tidy on this screen queues it for a run that has none, and is refused while any of the run's jobs is still queued or running. Once a run is packed the button reads Undo tidy, says how many files are in the archive, and unpacks them with a short queue job.

The timing record

The Jobs table lists every job the run has had, with its state, its node, its GPU or CPUs, its steps or frames, its wall time and, for a production part, its speed. Jobs a resubmission replaced are dimmed. Under it, one line gives the whole graph's time from the first start to the last end, the node time it used, and what the estimate said at Submit, so the two can be compared.

The record is kept in the run folder once every job has finished, and each finished job adds a row to the timing tables the next run's estimate reads: see The estimate. The tidy job records them too, so a run nobody opens is counted.
