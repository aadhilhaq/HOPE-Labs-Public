Analysis and MM-GBSA

When the production ends, one job measures every replicate's trajectory, and another scores the binding energy of the partner over a window of it that you choose.

What is measured

The analysis job runs on a CPU node after the last production segment and reads every part of every replicate with cpptraj, from the Amber module, on all five engines. For each replicate:

MeasureHow
Receptor RMSDOf the receptor's Cα atoms (P and C4′ for a nucleic acid), fitted to the first frame.
Partner or ligand RMSDOf its heavy atoms, with the receptor fitted and no fit of its own: how far it moved against the receptor.
Interface RMSDFor a protein or nucleic-acid partner: the traced atoms of each side within 8 Å of the other in the starting structure, with the receptor fitted.
Buried surfaceFor a protein or nucleic-acid partner: the receptor's surface and the partner's, less the complex's, frame by frame.
RMSFPer residue of the receptor, by its traced atoms.
Radius of gyrationOf the solute.
Contacts keptThe heavy-atom pairs of the partner and the receptor within 4.5 Å of each other in the first frame, and how many of them hold. A contact present in under half of the frames is lost.
Area per lipidIn a bilayer: see Reading a membrane run.

An apo run, with no partner, has the receptor's RMSD, its RMSF and its radius of gyration. The analysis also writes the trajectory the Runs screen's viewer plays: every 20th saved frame, without the water, the salt and any lipids.

On the Runs screen the measures are tiles, each with replicate 1's numbers and a plot; the report shows them the same way, with the contacts that were lost.

MM-GBSA

The binding energy of the partner or ligand over frames of the production: one job per replicate, after the production, on a CPU node. It is on by default for any run with a partner, and an apo run has nothing to score. The Analysis step sets the window:

FieldDefaultWhat it does
MM-GBSA binding energyonWhether the run scores it at all.
from (ns)0The first nanosecond of the production the frames come from. Skipping the first few leaves the relaxation out.
to (ns, 0 = end)0The last; 0 is the end of the run.
one frame every (ps)200One frame every this many picoseconds inside the window, no closer than the save interval. 200 ps over 10 ns is 50 frames, enough for the mean and its error; over a Standard run's 100 ns it is 500.

The line beside the fields counts the frames per replicate. Each replicate's result is the mean binding energy over its frames with its standard error; the report adds the standard deviation. MM-GBSA counts each ion the run keeps with the side whose residues bind it.

When the two sides are bonded

MM-GBSA scores the complex against the receptor and the partner taken apart. When a disulfide joins them, taking them apart cuts that bond: each side keeps a cysteine bonded to nothing, and the terms of the bond itself would go into the difference as if they were binding. That number is not a binding energy, so HOPE-MD does not compute it. The Analysis step says so in place of the MM-GBSA fields, no MM-GBSA job is queued, a job queued before Prepare found the bond stops at once with the reason in its log, MM-GBSA… on the Runs screen is refused, and the report says the two sides are covalently linked.

On Amber, OpenMM, GROMACS and NAMD

MMPBSA.py from AmberTools scores the frames on the run's own Amber topology, so the force field that ran the dynamics scores the binding. It uses the GB-Neck2 solvent model with its mbondi3 radii and the run's salt concentration, and it computes the energies through sander: MMPBSA.py's own code agrees with sander under GB-Neck2 on proteins, but not on phosphates or nucleic acids, whose parameters sander carries. The topologies of the complex, the receptor and the partner are made once, with the water and the salt stripped. On 8 CPUs, 500 frames of a replicate take about 3.5 minutes.

Prime MM-GBSA on Desmond

MMPBSA.py needs an Amber topology, and a Desmond run has none, so its MM-GBSA is Schrödinger's Prime: each frame is minimized and scored with OPLS4 and the VSGB 2.1 solvent model. Its numbers compare with other Prime numbers only.

  • It runs under the full Schrödinger suite, which needs your account on HPRC's Schrödinger users list.
  • Each Prime subjob holds 8 license tokens from HPRC's shared pool of 25, so the job runs one subjob. It scores a frame in 2.6 to 3.8 minutes, and its walltime follows its frames: a 50-frame window takes about 3 hours.
  • When fewer than 8 tokens are free, the job waits for them, counting every 5 minutes for up to 2 hours. If they are still taken, it ends red with that reason in its log, and MM-GBSA… on the Runs screen runs it again later.

A failed MM-GBSA does not hold the run back: tidy waits only for it to end, and the run can be scored again once tidied.

Reading the numbers

  • Compare runs scored the same way: MMPBSA.py's numbers with MMPBSA.py's, Prime's with Prime's.
  • For a DNA or RNA partner, the solvation cost of a polyanion is large and can outweigh the attraction of a basic protein, so the total may come out positive: read it as a trend between replicates and poses rather than as an absolute binding energy.
  • In a bilayer on the Amber-topology engines the lipids are stripped with the water, so the interface is scored in implicit solvent alone: read it as a trend between replicates, not as an energy. Prime, on Desmond, puts its own implicit membrane in their place.

Scoring again, over a new window

MM-GBSA… on the Runs screen scores a run that has its production over a window you set there, and works on a run submitted without MM-GBSA too: the stage joins its graph. The window goes into the run's spec, the MM-GBSA input is written again, and the job is submitted with everything after it. The result joins the tiles and the report when the job is done.
