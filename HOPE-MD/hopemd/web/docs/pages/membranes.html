Membranes

A membrane protein can run in a lipid bilayer in place of a water box. The structure has to stand in the membrane's frame, so the page finds its transmembrane segments and can orient it on them.

The bilayer

A bilayer takes NaCl or KCl and a rectangular box, the one shape periodic in x and y. Its fields on the Structure card:

FieldDefaultWhat it does
lipidPOPCThe lipid of the bilayer. POPC is the usual choice for a generic membrane.
water above and below (Å)10The water on each side of the bilayer, and the padding in x and y: 5 to 40 Å. Under 5 the head groups see their own image, and over 40 is water for nothing.
membrane center z (Å)0Where the bilayer's center sits along z: 0 for an OPM entry or a structure oriented on its segments.
orientationas importedHow the structure is put into the membrane's frame: see Orientation.
typed TM segmentsnoneSegments you know, as chain:start-end separated by commas, such as A:30-56, A:71-96: see Transmembrane segments.

A membrane run takes a protein, peptide or nucleic-acid partner, or none: a small molecule is not built into a bilayer here. Crystal waters are not built into one either, since the build places its own water around the solute, so set Prepare's waters to drop all, or the crystal waters to none for files. The run plan adds the number of lipids in the two leaflets to its estimate, and the viewer draws a slab where the bilayer will be.

Orientation

The bilayer is built in the xy plane, so the structure's membrane normal must be z, with the bilayer's center at the membrane center you give. Three ways to get there:

OrientationWhat happens
as importedThe coordinates are taken as they are: right for a structure already in the membrane's frame, such as a file from OPM.
fetch the OPM orientationFor an RCSB entry: the import fetches the entry's coordinates from OPM (opm.phar.umich.edu), which puts the membrane normal along z and its center at 0, in place of the RCSB's. The checks give the membrane's planes. Where OPM has no coordinates for the entry, the RCSB file is imported as deposited and the note says so: check the normal in the viewer.
from the transmembrane annotationThe structure is turned so the axis of its transmembrane segments is z and their center is at 0, when the segments are known: see Transmembrane segments.

The checks say which orientation the structure has, and warn when a bilayer is set along z as imported, so look at it in the viewer before you submit.

Transmembrane segments

Every import is searched for transmembrane segments, so the page can tell a membrane protein from a soluble one, in whichever environment you chose. In order, the search uses:

  1. UniProt's annotation, for an RCSB entry (or a file whose header names one), through PDBe's mapping of the entry's chains onto UniProt;
  2. UniProt's annotation for an AlphaFold job named after a UniProt accession;
  3. otherwise the best BLAST match in Swiss-Prot. The search runs at EBI, about a minute, and sends each protein chain of 30 residues or more, with your account name at tamu.edu as the contact address (HOPEMD_EMAIL on the server sets another). Meanwhile a hydrophobicity scan of the sequence stands in, and says it is a prediction: it finds helices, not beta barrels.

typed TM segments override all three and skip the search; Use these segments applies them to the checks, the coloring and, with the annotation as the orientation, the structure's orientation.

The viewer colors the segments sand, and TM in its bar shows or hides them. A protein counts as a membrane protein when segments land on the residues the structure holds, not when its UniProt entry merely carries the Membrane keyword, so a soluble domain cut from a membrane protein is not flagged. The checks warn when a membrane protein is set up in water, and Submit's notes say so again; they also warn when a bilayer is set up for a protein with no segment found.

Reading a membrane run

The analysis adds the area per lipid: the box's area in x and y over the lipids of one leaflet, frame by frame, the number that says the membrane has settled. It counts the solute's footprint in the area too, so it reads somewhat high, and its tile says so.

On the Amber-topology engines MM-GBSA strips the lipids with the water, so the interface is scored in implicit solvent alone: read it as a trend between replicates, not as an energy. Prime, on Desmond, puts its own implicit membrane in their place.
