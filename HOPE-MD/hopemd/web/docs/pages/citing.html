How to cite

HOPE-MD drives other people's engines and tools, and each run's report lists what that run used, with the references. Cite those, cite HOPE-MD, and acknowledge the computing.

What a run used

Open the run on the Runs screen and press Report. Its methods paragraph describes the run as its engine ran it, and cites as it goes; the references follow it, numbered in the order the paragraph cites them. They cover, as the run used them:

ForThe references name
the engineAmber 24 and pmemd.cuda, OpenMM 8, GROMACS, NAMD 3 or Desmond
the force fieldsff19SB, ff14SB or OPLS_2005; OL21, OL15 or parmbsc1 for DNA, and OL3 for RNA; Lipid17 in a bilayer
the water and the ionsOPC, TIP3P, TIP4P-Ew or SPC, and the 12-6 ion parameters that go with the water
a small moleculeGAFF2 with AM1-BCC charges
the dynamicshydrogen mass repartitioning at 4 fs; the thermostat and barostat on GROMACS, and the barostat on NAMD and Desmond
the preparationPDBFixer, and PDB2PQR with PROPKA, where they ran
the analysiscpptraj; ParmEd for a GROMACS topology
the binding energyMMPBSA.py, or Prime MM-GBSA with VSGB

The report's Protocol table gives the module line each job loaded, so the versions are the cluster's, and the run's README.txt names them too. Check each reference against the version the jobs loaded before it goes into a manuscript, as the report itself says.

If a figure from the viewer is used

A figure made in the page's viewer cites the viewer that drew it, and MSMS when it shows a surface: the surfaces the 3Dmol.js viewers draw, on Setup and on Runs, are computed by MSMS. A surface the viewer drew itself, where MSMS gave none, says so in the viewer, and a figure of it cites the viewer alone. Each run's report lists these after its references, and the page's How to cite gives them too.

If the run used NAMD

NAMD is not open source, and its license asks for more than a citation: an acknowledgment, in these words, in anything that reports work it did.

NAMD was developed by the Theoretical and Computational Biophysics Group in the Beckman Institute for Advanced Science and Technology at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.

A run on any of the other four engines does not need it. The report of a run that used NAMD carries the sentence, so it is in front of you when you write the methods up rather than something to remember.

HOPE-MD itself

Cite HOPE-MD from the CITATION.cff in its repository, which is what GitHub's Cite this repository reads, with the version you ran. The bar along the bottom of the page shows the version and the commit being served, and the last sentence of a report's methods names the version and commit that set the run up.

HOPE-MD is written by Aadhil Haq, Samavath Mallawarachchi and Lasan Manujitha in the HOPE Lab of Dr. Sandun Fernando, principal investigator, Department of Biological and Agricultural Engineering, Texas A&M University, which holds the copyright. The bar along the bottom of every page states it, word for word as the repository's LICENSE has it, and AUTHORS.md beside it gives each author's contributions.

The computing

Runs on Grace use Texas A&M's computing. The launcher prints this sentence when it connects, and the page's How to cite gives it too, for the acknowledgments of anything that used them:

Please acknowledge Texas A&M High Performance Research Computing (HPRC) for the computational resources.

Work run on ACES acknowledges the computing as ACCESS asks, naming ACES at Texas A&M HPRC and your allocation; ACES is funded by NSF award 2112356.
