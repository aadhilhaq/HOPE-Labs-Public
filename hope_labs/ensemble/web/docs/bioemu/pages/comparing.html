Against a simulation

Point a finished ensemble at a HOPE-MD run of the same chain and ask whether the two agree. What is measured, what the numbers mean, and what neither of them proves.

Why this is here

Minutes against days, on the same protein. If the sampled ensemble holds the shapes the simulation visits, the sampling can be trusted for the next protein and a great deal of card time is saved. If it does not, that is worth knowing before a week of simulation is planned on its suggestion. Either way the comparison is the thing that turns a sampled ensemble from an interesting picture into evidence.

What you need

A finished run on this page, read, and a HOPE-MD run whose complex holds a chain with the same sequence. The chain is matched on its sequence rather than on its name or its length: HOPE-MD renames chains as it prepares a complex, and the two chains of a dimer have the same length. If no chain matches, the comparison refuses and lists the sequences it found, so you can see at once whether you pointed it at the wrong run or at a construct with a tag on it.

The trajectory is read if it is there, and the viewer export (analysis/r1_view.pdb) if it is not, which is what a tidied HOPE-MD run looks like. The comparison says which of the two it read and how many frames, because the answer is worth less from a few hundred frames than from a few thousand.

What is measured

The shapes, one row each. For every sampled shape, the closest the simulation ever came to it, and whether that is within 0.3 nm. This is the headline: a sampled shape the simulation came within 0.3 nm of is a shape both methods agree the protein takes.

The residues. The fluctuation of each residue in each, and the correlation between the two. This is the number that says whether the two agree about which parts of the chain move, which is often more useful than agreement about the shapes themselves. Above about 0.7 they agree closely; below about 0.4 they are telling different stories and the reason is worth chasing.

The spread. The radius of gyration of each, mean and width, and how much the two distributions overlap, as the common area of their histograms. One for a pair that sit on top of one another, zero for a pair that do not touch.

Reading the answer

Most shapes visited, residues correlated, spreads overlapping. The two agree. Quote both, and use the sampling for the next protein in the series.

The populated shapes visited, a small one not. The usual result, and not a disagreement: a simulation of a few hundred nanoseconds has not visited everything, and a shape holding two per cent of the samples is exactly the kind of thing it would miss.

The simulation visits something that was not sampled. More interesting. Either the sampling missed a populated state, which is a real limitation of the model, or the simulation went somewhere the protein does not go, which happens: a forcefield artifact, an unfolding, a bad start. Look at the structure before deciding which.

Nothing matches at all. Check first that both are the same chain, in the same protonation and without a tag, and that the simulation had settled before the frames being read. A comparison against the first nanoseconds of a run is a comparison against the model building rather than against the protein.

What neither of them proves

A simulation is not an experiment and neither is a sampled ensemble. Two methods agreeing is two models agreeing, which is worth more than one model on its own and is not the same as being right. Where the answer matters, it is worth saying what the experimental evidence is, even when it is only that the fold is the one in the structure both started from.

How it runs

As a short processor job on the ordinary partition, because it reads every frame of the trajectory and nothing it does wants a card. It writes into the ensemble run's own folder, under compare/, named after the simulation, so one ensemble can be compared with several simulations and each answer kept. Nothing is ever written into the HOPE-MD run.
