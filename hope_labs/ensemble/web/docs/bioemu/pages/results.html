What a run leaves

The files in a finished run folder, what each is for, and which of them you want when you take the results somewhere else.

The folder

sequence.fasta            the sequence as you gave it
submission.json           what was asked for, and what the job was told
job.sbatch                the job script, exactly as it was queued
slurm_<job>.out           the job's own log
inputs/alignment.a3m      the alignment phase one fetched
out/samples.xtc           the samples, as one trajectory file
out/topology.pdb          the topology they are read with
out/batch_*.npz           the per batch arrays, packed away when the run tidies itself
analysis/analysis.json    everything reading the samples found
analysis/ensemble.pdb     one model per shape, most populated first
analysis/rg.dat           every sample: radius of gyration, distance from the mean shape
analysis/rmsf.dat         every residue: how much it moves about the mean shape
compare/<run>/compare.json  a comparison against one HOPE-MD run

The samples themselves

A trajectory file and a topology, which is how every tool that reads ensembles expects them: MDTraj, MDAnalysis, VMD, PyMOL and the rest will all open out/samples.xtc with out/topology.pdb. The file format is the one simulations use because it is the efficient way to hold many structures of one molecule, and not because what is in it is a trajectory. The shapes, and what they are not is the page about that.

The model writes a backbone and one side chain atom per residue. That is enough for a cartoon, for secondary structure and for every number on this page, and it is not a full atom structure: nothing here can tell you about a side chain rotamer.

What reading them found

analysis/analysis.json holds the lot, and it is the file the page, the report and a flow all read:

  • the spread: the radius of gyration of each sample, with its mean and width, and the distance of each sample from the mean shape;
  • the shapes: the groups the samples fall into, each with the share of the samples it holds, how wide it is, and which sample is its representative;
  • the residues: how much each one moves about the mean shape, and what share of the samples holds it as helix, as sheet or as neither;
  • the sequence read back out of the structures, which is the check that the run sampled what you think it sampled.

The structures to show somebody

analysis/ensemble.pdb is one model per shape, up to eight of them, most populated first, aligned onto one another. It is what the viewer draws and what belongs in a figure: eight structures that are each a real draw from the model, rather than an average of anything.

A shape holding less than one sample in a hundred is left out of that file. It is still in analysis.json, with its share, so nothing is hidden.

Taking the results somewhere

Copy, on the run's screen, writes into a folder on the cluster rather than through your browser, because the lab's work stays on Grace. Three choices:

  • the shapes: ensemble.pdb and analysis.json, which is what a slide or a colleague needs;
  • the ensemble: those two, the samples, the topology and the per sample files, which is what another analysis needs;
  • the run: the whole folder.

Copying into your home folder is refused: home on this cluster is 10 GB and 10,000 files.
