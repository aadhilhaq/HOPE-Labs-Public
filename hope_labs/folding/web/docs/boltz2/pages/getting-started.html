Getting started

One run from an empty form to a structure, with the waiting explained.

Opening the page

Sign in to HOPE Labs, find Boltz-2 in the Tools list and press Launch. The launcher starts the page on a login node, forwards a port to your own computer and opens it. The page carries a token in its address: that is what makes it yours on a shared login node, so open it from the launcher rather than keeping the address.

Filling in the form

The form is two cards and a column down the right. The card to do next is outlined, and the bar along the bottom says in one line what to do, with Show me to take you there.

  1. What to fold. Press Choose a structure. It opens the structure browser: this computer, your scratch, home, this page's runs, the other tools' run folders and your group's folders, and a PDB code. Pick a file and its chains are read off it and become rows, protein and nucleic acid alike, with the copies worked out. The structure's chips say which chains are on the form: press one to leave that chain out, and again to bring it back. A FASTA chosen in the same browser becomes one row per record. A file dropped on the card is read the same way.
  2. Or press Paste sequences and paste plain letters or FASTA, or PDB code and type four characters. Each row is a chain or a molecule: a protein, a DNA or RNA strand, a ligand or an ion. A dimer of one sequence is one row with two copies; two different chains are two rows. + Add a molecule under the rows adds one by hand: choose its type, then type or paste its sequence, or pick the ligand or ion.
  3. Settings. Leave them alone unless you have a reason. They are Boltz-2's own defaults, and the reason for each is behind its ?. More options holds the ones few people change.
  4. The column on the right. Resources is the GPU, the time limit and the account; Where it goes is the run's name and folder, both chosen for you if you leave them. Once there is something to fold, the run plan appears above Submit: what will happen, where, and roughly how long. Show the job under it opens a YAML file exactly as it will be written, chain ids and all, which is worth a look: a copy count or a chain id read wrong does not fail, it folds something else for a few hours and reports a confidence score for it.
  5. Press Submit. When it cannot be pressed yet, the line under it says why.

The text size

The − 100% + control in the header makes the page's text and controls smaller or larger, from 80% to 150%, and pressing the percentage goes back to 100%. The page remembers your choice. The structure viewers keep their size, so a residue clicked is the residue under the pointer at any text size.

The wait after Submit

Submit does two things. First it fetches the alignments, here on the login node, and that takes a minute or two per distinct protein sequence, longer when the alignment server is busy. The button says so while it is happening. Then it writes the job and queues it, and the page moves to the Runs screen.

If you would rather not wait at all, set Alignments to Sequence only. Nothing is fetched, the job is queued at once, and the structures are markedly worse. It is the right choice for a quick look and the wrong one for anything you intend to keep.

Trying the page without spending a card

Dry run writes the run folder, a YAML file and the job script, fetches no alignments and queues nothing. It is how to see exactly what a run would be told before asking for a GPU, and it is how this page is worked on.

Then what

The Runs screen shows the job's state and the log while it runs. When it finishes, the structures are listed best first and the top one is drawn in the viewer. See what a run leaves and looking at a structure.
