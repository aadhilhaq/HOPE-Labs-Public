How to cite

The folding is not the lab's work. These are the papers and the licenses of the people whose work a run on this page actually does.

Boltz-2

The folding is Boltz-2's. Cite it as its authors ask:

Passaro, S., Corso, G., Wohlwend, J., Reveiz, M., Thaler, S., Somnath, V. R.,
Getz, N., Portnoi, T., Roy, J., Stark, H., Kwabi-Addo, D., Beaini, D., Jaakkola, T.,
and Barzilay, R. Boltz-2: Towards Accurate and Efficient Binding Affinity Prediction.
bioRxiv (2025). doi:10.1101/2025.06.14.659707

Its authors also ask that Boltz-1 be cited alongside it:

Wohlwend, J., Corso, G., Passaro, S., Getz, N., Reveiz, M., Leidal, K.,
Swiderski, W., Atkinson, L., Portnoi, T., Chinn, I., Silterra, J., Jaakkola, T.,
and Barzilay, R. Boltz-1: Democratizing Biomolecular Interaction Modeling.
bioRxiv (2024). doi:10.1101/2024.11.19.624167

Boltz-2 is released by its authors under the MIT license, with the copyright held by Jeremy Wohlwend, Gabriele Corso and Saro Passaro. The license text is in the install, in the package's own dist-info folder.

The alignments

Unless you folded from the sequence alone, the alignments came from the ColabFold MSA server, and Boltz-2's authors ask that it be cited when it is used:

Mirdita, M., Schütze, K., Moriwaki, Y., Heo, L., Ovchinnikov, S., and
Steinegger, M. ColabFold: making protein folding accessible to all.
Nature Methods 19, 679 to 682 (2022). doi:10.1038/s41592-022-01488-1

The server is a shared resource with a daily limit, which is worth knowing: it is run as a courtesy by people who are not being paid for your run.

The viewers

If a figure came out of the viewer on the Runs screen:

Rego, N. and Koes, D. 3Dmol.js: molecular visualization with WebGL.
Bioinformatics 31, 1322 to 1324 (2015). doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btu829
Sehnal, D. et al. Mol* Viewer: modern web app for 3D visualization and analysis
of large biomolecular structures. Nucleic Acids Research 49, W431 to W437 (2021).
doi:10.1093/nar/gkab314

A run from this page can also be opened in HOPE Labs' own viewer, under Results. A figure made there cites 3Dmol.js, as above, and one that shows a surface cites MSMS as well, which computed the surface:

Sanner, M. F., Olson, A. J. and Spehner, J.-C. Reduced surface: an efficient way to
compute molecular surfaces. Biopolymers 38, 305 to 320 (1996).
doi:10.1002/(SICI)1097-0282(199603)38:3<305::AID-BIP4>3.0.CO;2-Y
