The sequences

The rows, what is checked in them, and why the checking is on this page rather than left to the tool.

One row per chain

A folding run is a list of entities. Each row is a type, an optional name, a sequence and a number of copies. The copies are chains of the same sequence: a row of 300 residues with three copies is three chains and 900 residues, and the running total under the rows says so.

The name is yours, for reading the run folder later. It is not the chain id.

The types

TypeLettersNotes
Proteinthe twenty standard amino acids, and X One chain per row. The chains are written into one FASTA record, joined by colons, which is how ColabFold is told that they belong in one structure.

There is no other type, because ColabFold has no other model. A nucleic acid pasted in is refused with a sentence saying so rather than folded as a protein.

Why the letters are checked here

Neither folding tool refuses a sequence it does not understand. ColabFold maps every character it has no residue for to its unknown token and folds what is left, so a lowercase sequence becomes a chain of nothing but unknown residues and comes back with a confidence score on it. That is the worst kind of failure, because it looks like a result.

So the sequence box marks every letter the tool has no residue for, where it is, and the run is refused until they are gone. Lower case is simply made upper case, because case carries no meaning in a sequence. A gap character is refused with a message of its own: it usually means a row of an alignment has been pasted instead of a sequence.

Chain ids

Chain ids are handed out A, B, C in row order, one per copy, and the page shows which letters each row became. That is the order the tool names its chains in, so the ids on the page are the ids in the structure that comes back. Twenty-six chains is the limit, counting copies.

Pasting sequences, and FASTA files

Paste sequences takes a bare sequence or FASTA and makes one row per record, with the name from the header. The type is read off the letters where the letters settle it: a U with no T is RNA, a T with no U is DNA. A sequence of nothing but A, C and G is a protein, a DNA strand and an RNA strand all at once, so it is read as a protein and the page says that it guessed. Nothing is decided quietly.

A FASTA file is chosen in the structure browser, like a structure: on the cluster, or under This computer, which sends it up through the page. A .fasta, .fa, .faa, .txt or .seq file becomes one row per record, as a paste does. Two megabytes is the most the page takes from your computer, which is about two million residues; a sequence file is kilobytes, so anything near the cap is something else. A file on the cluster is read where it is, and nothing crosses the connection.

A structure pasted into Paste sequences, or a structure file given where sequences are read, is refused with a pointer to Choose a structure rather than read as letters: it used to become one row of fifty thousand characters, refused for holding a digit.

Choosing a structure

Choose a structure reads the chains off a file and makes them rows: a crystal structure, a fold this page wrote last week, a docked pose, a prepared complex out of a HOPE-MD run. It is there because the alternative is opening the file in a viewer and copying the one-letter codes out by hand, and a chain copied by eye with one residue missed is the one mistake on this form that does not fail. It folds, and it comes back with a confidence score on it.

The button opens the structure browser, the same one every page of HOPE Labs uses. Down its left are the places a structure can come from:

Choosing the chains

Once a structure is read it has a summary on the card: its name, how it arrived, Change to choose another in its place, and a chip for every chain in the file with its kind and length. The pressed chips are the chains on the form, and every chain that can be folded starts pressed. Press one to leave that chain out, and again to bring it back: a receptor and the peptide bound to it are usually two rows, and sometimes only one of the two is wanted. Two chains of one sequence become one row with two copies rather than two rows, whether one of their chips is pressed or both, so 4INS is four chains and two rows.

What is left out, and why

Nothing is dropped without a word. A chain that cannot become a row has a dashed chip, the reason is its tooltip, and a line under the chips names it. Pressing a dashed chip copies the chain's sequence, so it can be put right by hand:

  • A nucleic acid chain. The chains are read off the file by their backbone, which is the alpha carbon of an amino acid or the C1' of a nucleotide, so a DNA or an RNA strand is read with its bases and then refused: ColabFold folds protein chains only. Folded as a protein its A, C, G and T would be read as alanine, cysteine, glycine and threonine, and the run would finish and put a confidence score on the result. The Boltz-2 page folds a strand, and reads one off this same file.
  • A chain holding residues that are not one of the twenty amino acids: a ligand or a sugar numbered into the chain, or a residue nothing here has a letter for. Its letters carry an X at each one.
  • A chain with no backbone atom at all, which is how a ligand, an ion or a sugar given a chain of its own appears. It is in the file, it has no sequence to read, so it is named and left.
  • A chain shorter than four residues, or longer than the most this page will fold.

Water is not a chain here and is never counted as one. Nor is a lone metal or salt ion, which a structure writes as a residue of its own: a calcium ion is written with CA for both its atom name and its residue name, so counting residues by that name alone once made every ion in a calmodulin a residue of the chain beside it.

A chain with a gap

A chain whose numbering jumps says so on its chip, as 1 gap: residues are missing from the file there, and the fold joins the residues either side as neighbors. It is said rather than closed silently, because a chain with a loop missing is a different molecule from the one the file describes; a structure with the loop built in is the one to fold.

What the run remembers

A row read off a structure keeps the file and the chain with it. The line under the row says 1ycr.cif chain A, the finished run says it again under that chain, and the report's summary names the file. Change the letters and the line goes: a record must not name a file and a chain for a sequence that is no longer in that file.

How large a run may be

Five thousand residues in all is the most this page will queue, and over about fifteen hundred it says so: both tools hold something that grows with the square of the residue count, so what runs out is the card rather than the clock. Show the job under the run plan names the card and how much memory it has, and Resources and submitting says which cards this cluster has.
