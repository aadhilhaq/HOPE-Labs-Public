Looking at a structure

The two viewers, what the colors mean, and why both are bundled with the page.

The viewer

When a run has finished, its structures are listed best first and the top one is drawn below the table. Press View on any row to draw that one instead. The files are PDB, and both viewers read them. In 3Dmol.js, click a residue and the bar over the viewer names it: its chain, its number and its pLDDT. Mol* labels what is under the pointer itself.

3Dmol.js is the default: it is small, it loads at once, and it is enough for looking at a fold and turning it over. Mol* is the other choice in the menu beside the viewer. It is five megabytes, so it is fetched the first time it is asked for and then kept, and it is worth asking for when you want its sequence panel or a closer look at an interface.

Why they are bundled

Both viewers are files in this page rather than addresses on the internet. The page is served on a login node and reached through a forwarded port, so a browser looking at it can fetch from the page and from nowhere else. A viewer loaded from a content delivery network would be an empty box. The versions and the licenses are recorded in the page's own vendor folder.

The colors

The structure is colored by pLDDT, the model's own confidence in each residue, because that is what a person opens a predicted structure to see. The bands are AlphaFold's own, and so are the colors, so the scale is the one you have seen before.

ColorpLDDTWhat it means
dark blueabove 90very high confidence; the backbone and usually the side chains can be trusted
light blue70 to 90confident; the backbone is probably right
yellow50 to 70low; treat the local geometry with suspicion
orangebelow 50very low; often a genuinely disordered region, and should not be interpreted as a structure

The confidence is read out of the B-factor column of the file, which is where both tools write it. The scale is worked out from the file rather than from which tool wrote it, because ColabFold writes pLDDT out of a hundred and Boltz-2 writes it out of one, and a scale read the wrong way round would color a confident model as a hopeless one.

A ligand or a nucleic acid has no cartoon to draw, so it is drawn as sticks in the same colors. A region that is orange in a complex is frequently a linker or a terminus rather than a failure, and a whole chain that is orange usually means the alignment was poor or absent.
