Flows

Draw the chain once, fill in the target, launch the whole of it. The cluster runs it card by card whether the launcher is open or not.

Drawing one

Drag a card from the list on the left onto the canvas and join the sockets. A link the tools cannot make is refused as you draw it rather than when it is queued; a link that needs a preparation gets one, put in as you draw it, and the panel on the right fills in the card you have picked. Where a flow has a target, it is usually the only card that needs anything: everything joined to it takes the same receptor and the same hotspots, so the design, the docking and the simulation are all aimed at one site.

The receptor reaches each card in the form that card can use. The docking cards (ADCP docking, HADDOCK docking) are given it prepared, once, by a Prepare receptor card that the canvas puts between the target and them. The design tools (BindCraft2, RFdiffusion, RFdiffusion3 and BoltzGen) are given the target cleaned instead, never protonated, so a design card cannot be linked to a prepared receptor at all. Why each is given what it is given is under The receptor.

The hotspots are asked for only where something in the flow aims at a site: the design tools, ADCP docking, and HADDOCK docking while it is driven by the interface. A blind docking, a co-fold and a simulation have no site, so they need none, and a flow with no site-aimed card in it needs no target card at all. Boltz-2 co-folding a receptor with the strand or peptide that binds it, prepared and simulated, is a whole flow on its own.

A fold can go to three places. Into the target, a folded protein becomes the receptor. Into HADDOCK docking, each fold that arrives becomes one more partner, docked onto the target in a job of its own, and HADDOCK hands on the best model of each partner's best clusters. Into HOPE-MD, a co-folded complex is simulated as it was folded, with no docking in the flow at all. On the two links that do not go to the target a preparation stands in the middle, because nothing is docked or simulated unprepared: see below. A simulation with DNA or RNA in it takes the engine's usual nucleic-acid force field unless the HOPE-MD card names another.

Boltz-2 folds one chain or several. One sequence gives a structure for a target card to take; fill in a second sequence, and its kind, and the two are folded together as a complex (a receptor with the DNA, RNA or peptide that binds it), which goes on to be prepared and simulated with no docking in the flow at all. Ask for more than one structure and every sample is handed on, best first, for the card below to take the best few of. A complex of more than three different chains is a job for the Boltz-2 page itself.

Numbers have arrows and the range the tool actually allows. A box left empty where empty is a real answer says in grey what will happen instead, which is usually the tool's own default. A field the card answers for itself, such as the timestep of an engine that fixes its own, is shown with the reason rather than left to be filled in and ignored.

Preparation, which is not optional

A predicted or docked structure is coordinates, not chemistry: its histidines have no protonation state, its termini are not settled, its disulfides are unmeasured and its gaps are holes. So every link that carries a structure into a docking card or into HOPE-MD has a Prepare card in the middle of it, and every link from a target into a docking card has the Prepare receptor card. The canvas puts them there as you draw the link, and neither has a × to take it off: each belongs to its link rather than being a card of its own. Take the link off to be rid of it.

It splits what arrives into the receptor and the partner, prepares each on its own (one pH for the whole flow, protonation, termini, disulfides, gaps), puts them back together and checks the net charge. A receptor or partner of several chains is prepared whole, so a dimer held by disulfides between its chains keeps them. Preparing each molecule separately is the point: protonating an assembled complex answers those questions for the interface as well, which is the one place the answer depends on how the two molecules happen to have been put together. The work is HOPE-MD's own Prepare stage, run once per molecule, and HOPE-MD is then told the structure is already prepared so that it does not do it again over the assembled complex.

A gap is found from the sequence the file records (its SEQRES), so a deposited structure has its missing loops built back up to the longest the card allows, 12 at most, and a predicted model, which has no gaps, has none to build. They are built as ADCP builds them: only a gap inside a chain, never a chain's missing ends; from a fixed seed, so the same structure gives the same loop; relaxed with the residues either side on springs; and every join checked. A loop that cannot be made to fit stops that structure's preparation, and the message says to lower Longest gap to model. A built residue takes the number of the missing residue, or an insertion code where that number is taken. A gap longer than the limit is left open and its two ends are capped: an NME on the residue before it and an ACE on the residue after it, so neither end is a charged terminus the protein does not have. The caps take the numbers of the missing residues next to the gap (a gap between A:190 and A:195 gets NME A:191 and ACE A:194), or insertion codes where no number is free (thrombin's H:149A-149E gets NME H:149B and ACE H:149D), and no residue is renumbered, so hotspots name the residues they named before; a hotspot on a residue that is still missing is refused, cap or no cap. With Cap the breaks off, each piece ends in charged termini instead, an OXT before the gap and NH3+ after it. A structure that has been prepared before has its hydrogens and the OXT at each break taken off first, and keeps the caps it has, so preparing it again gives the same chains, caps and states.

What it decided is written beside the flow, in the card's own folder, as prepared.json: for every molecule its protonation states, its termini, its disulfides (including any that hold two molecules together), the gaps modelled and left, and its charge; and for every structure the net charge and what makes it up. Files on the card opens it.

What becomes of the waters, ions, metals and cofactors depends on the card below. Into HOPE-MD they ride through the preparation at their own coordinates, and which to keep and which side of the interface each belongs to is settled by HOPE-MD's own Prepare, where it always was. Into a docking card they do not: every water and every hetero group is dropped, a co-crystallised inhibitor included, except the single-atom ions that docking can use (HADDOCK's). What was dropped is in prepared.json and in the card's log.

So the five boxes about them are on the Prepare card and not on Prepare receptor: Crystal waters, Keep metals and cofactors, Drop the crystallisation buffers, Keep the hetero groups that are neither and Also drop, by residue name. Each is read where the decision is made, over the assembled complex, so a group that no force field here covers is named in the refusal rather than dropped on the way without a word, and Also drop, by residue name is what that refusal asks you for. The receptor card has none of them, because for a receptor the answer is already settled.

Three more boxes are about the structure as it arrives, and both cards have them. Trim predicted tails below pLDDT takes the terminal residues a predicted model has no confidence in, which is the first thing to come off one; 50 is HOPE-MD's own threshold, 0 keeps every residue, and a structure with no pLDDT in it is untouched. Alternate location to keep chooses between the positions a crystal structure models for the same side chain. Model to take chooses within an NMR ensemble. The minimisation has its own two: how hard the atoms the file already had are held (Minimisation restraint, so only the modelled residues move) and whether it runs in implicit solvent rather than vacuum, which is a better answer for a modelled loop and about ten times slower.

The receptor

A target linked to a docking card goes through one Prepare receptor card. There is one per target, shared by every docking card the target feeds, so everything one flow docks is docked against the same structure. It takes the target's own chains and no others, and prepares them as one molecule with HOPE-MD's Prepare: protonation by propka at the flow's pH, AMBER residue names, termini and disulfides, and gaps up to the longest the card allows built back from the file's SEQRES. The waters and hetero groups go, as into any docking. Each docking card is then told that its receptor is prepared, and at which pH, so that it does not prepare it again its own way. Where a docking card has a pH box of its own, it shows the flow's pH, set for you: one flow has one pH.

The hotspots are checked against the prepared receptor, residue by residue. Each is present (the same chain, number, insertion code and residue, a protonation-state name such as HID apart), built (a residue the file was missing and the preparation modelled back) or missing. A hotspot on a built residue is flagged on the card, because a site on a modelled loop is a site on a guess. A missing one stops every docking card that aims at the site, with the residue named, rather than letting it dock somewhere else.

The design tools are not given a prepared receptor. The names a preparation gives (HID, CYX, ASH) and the hydrogens it adds crash some of them and are read as something else by the rest. So the target card writes a cleaned copy for them: heavy atoms only, HID, HIE and HIP back to HIS, CYX to CYS, ASH to ASP, GLH to GLU and LYN to LYS, no waters, hetero groups or caps, one alternate location of each atom, and an OXT only on the last residue of a chain. The chain letters, the residue numbers and the insertion codes are kept exactly as they were, because the hotspots are written in them.

What the docking cards can be told

Each docking card offers the settings its own tool offers, with that tool's bounds and that tool's defaults, so a flow docks the way the tool's own page docks. A box left empty where empty is a real answer says in grey what the tool will do instead. The few below are the ones worth knowing about, and they are all under More settings unless said otherwise.

HADDOCK docking is driven by residues, and both sides can name them. The target's hotspots are the receptor's active residues, as they always were; Partners' active residues is the same thing on the partner, for a known epitope, and each of the first three partners has a box of its own, as does each partner's list of chains, so a protein partner and a two-chain DNA duplex can be docked in one card. A partner with no box of its own takes the first partner's. The passive residues of either side follow the active ones unless you say otherwise: the surface around them where a molecule names any, its whole surface where it names none. Name residues on the partner alone and the docking is still driven by residues, with the receptor's whole surface passive. Name none anywhere, with the hotspots unticked, and it is ab initio.

The rest of that card is HADDOCK's own advanced options, which a flow could not reach at all: Rescore the top poses with MM-GBSA, which is the only way to compare one partner with another, since HADDOCK's own score was made to rank the models of one run; the engines that score them and how many poses; Refine the final models in water, which HADDOCK's own page ticks for a DNA or RNA partner and which a flow therefore used to lose; the clustering, its cutoff and the smallest cluster kept; a sampling of your own past the three presets; a seed, and docking each partner more than once with a seed apiece; a restraints file of your own; a nucleic acid's secondary structure as dot-bracket, whose base pairs are then held through every refinement; a peptide partner closed into a ring; the residues HADDOCK may move freely; a known complex to compare the models with; and the ions and cofactors to keep by name. The memory, the Slurm throttle and any other Slurm options are there too.

ADCP docking can dock a peptide as a ring. Dock each peptide as takes a linear chain, a ring closed head to tail, or a ring through a disulfide between two cysteines; a peptide that cannot close the ring asked for is left behind by name, with the reason, and the rest are docked. Build the box around is where the grid box comes from: the hotspots, which is what it has always been; a ligand or peptide already in the receptor, named by its chain or as A:NUT:201, which is the surest way to dock into the site something is already sitting in; or the pocket AutoSite finds. The box's own padding and its smallest and largest size are settings of the tool's page and its job file and not of its command line, so they are not offered here. The rescoring's solvation model, its engine, how many poses it scores and how many it reports the mean of are all on the card, as are the share of replicas that start as a helix, the poses minimised with OpenMM afterwards, the AutoSite version, the receptor gradient, keeping every replica's trajectory, and the limit on how many peptides are docked at once. ADCP names no partition of its own, so an empty Partition leaves the choice to the scheduler.

The target, and where its structure comes from

A PDB id is fetched on a login node when the flow is queued, so a wrong one is refused while you are still looking at the page. Which copy to fetch is the asymmetric unit, as deposited, or a biological assembly built from the coordinates and the symmetry: the assembly is the right answer wherever the biological unit is not the deposited one, a dimer deposited as one chain and a symmetry mate. The asymmetric unit stays the default, because it is what the card has always fetched.

An entry with no PDB file is fetched as mmCIF and converted, which is the only way such an entry can be used by its id at all: 4V60, 5J7V, 7A01, 6QZ9 and 8GLV have no PDB file, and an assembly comes only as mmCIF for every entry. One format leaves this card, and it is PDB, because everything under it reads PDB. A structure past what that format holds, 99,999 atoms or 62 chains, is refused with its size named rather than written out with atom numbers no reader here understands. Chains is the way through: the chains named on the card are taken out of the mmCIF before it is converted, so two chains of a ribosome can be the target even though the whole of it cannot.

Hotspots are written once, as 54,56,66-70 or A54,B12-16, and each card is given them in the form its own tool reads: a colon between the chain and the number where the tool wants one, every residue of a range written out, and an insertion code kept for the tools that read one and dropped with a word said for the tools that key a residue by its number alone. Chains and the binder length are written once as well and reach every card below.

What the folding cards can be told

Both folding cards offer their own page's rail, with that page's bounds and that page's defaults. Boltz-2 has Structures to fold, which hands every sample on, best first; Recycling steps and Sampling steps, which cost time roughly in proportion; and a Seed, which is what makes a fold repeatable: without one a structure cannot be produced twice, and a model nobody can reproduce is a model nobody can argue about. Steering potentials is off, as Boltz-2 has it, and on is slower and reported upstream to give structures that make better physical sense.

Modified residues takes a list as 12 SEP, 40 TPO: the residue's position in the sequence above it and the code it becomes. Each chain of a co-fold has its own box. A code that is not a modification of the residue it is put on is refused as the flow is queued, with the residue and the code named, because Boltz-2 itself would put a phosphoserine wherever it was told. ColabFold has no box of this kind, and that is not an omission: its input is a FASTA, which has nowhere to carry one.

ColabFold has Models and Recycling steps, and under More settings Seeds, which is more samples per model and more time in proportion, and Rank by, which decides which model is handed on as the best: auto is AlphaFold-Multimer's own measure for a complex and pLDDT for a single chain. There is no seed box, because ColabFold takes a number of seeds rather than a seed.

A fold leaves PDB, not the mmCIF the Boltz-2 page prefers, and neither card offers the choice. What leaves a folding card becomes a target, and every tool a target reaches reads PDB.

One run per structure, and which structures

Everything that reaches HOPE-MD is one run per structure, and the card says which. What arrives is ranked across all of a track's partners together, by the score the tool that made them gave them, so the best five of a docking of three partners are the best five it docked and not the five of whichever partner was named first. Which of what arrives to simulate is by rank, which takes that many off the top, or by name, which takes the ones you name whatever their rank: the names are the ones the preparation gives them, a docking's <partner>_cluster<n>, a fold's <card>_model<n>, a peptide's <peptide>_pose<n>. A name nothing answers to is said in the card's log rather than passed over.

One structure can be given its own settings without a second card. Settings for one structure, under More settings, takes a group per structure as name: key=value, key=value, with a semicolon between groups, and the keys are this card's own: 3cc1_cluster2: length_ns=200, engine=openmm runs that one structure for longer on another engine and leaves the rest of the batch alone. A key the card has not got, or a number outside what it allows, is refused before anything is queued.

Every box on that card is bounded by what HOPE-MD will actually run, and the pairs it insists on are made for you: an engine that fixes its timestep shows it fixed, and the force field, the water and the box shape follow the engine unless you name them, so a Desmond run is written with OPLS, a three-site water and an orthorhombic box rather than refused for asking for the others. Replicates stop at five, the timestep is 2 fs or 4, and a frame count whose interval is not a whole number of steps, or is longer than the 200 ps MM-GBSA takes its frames at, is refused on the canvas with the number to type instead. Account to charge reaches every job of every run, the preparation job included.

Frames to keep is 5000, which over the 100 ns the card asks for by default is a frame every 20 ps: HOPE-MD's own interval, and the one all three of its presets use. The number on the card is a count and the interval is the length divided by it, so a longer run needs more frames; the canvas says which number to type. A run of three replicates of a 30 000-atom complex is about 5.5 GB of trajectory at that interval, which is what Tidy each run when it finishes is for.

The box, the salt and the bilayer

Box shape and Padding are the water box: a truncated octahedron by default, because it holds the fewest waters for the same distance between periodic images, with 12 Å between the solute and the edge. The padding cannot go under the 10 Å cutoff, or the complex would see its own image. Salt and Salt concentration neutralise the box and then bring it to a concentration, 150 mM NaCl by default; MgCl2 is there for a nucleic acid, which binds Mg2+. The concentration is also the salt MM-GBSA is computed at, so it changes the binding energy as well as the box, which is why a nucleic-acid run is worth setting it for.

What it is simulated in is a water box or a lipid bilayer. A membrane protein in a water box is not a slower answer but the wrong one, and until this box existed a flow could only ask for water. A bilayer is tiled from a pre-equilibrated patch of the Lipid chosen, with Water above and below the bilayer on each face and its centre at Bilayer centre along z, which is 0 by the OPM convention. A bilayer build fixes the protein force field, the water and the box shape, and the card writes all three for you as it does Desmond's. What it cannot write for you is the salt, because quietly swapping MgCl2 for NaCl would change the chemistry of the run: a bilayer takes a monovalent pair, and the canvas says so. Crystal waters cannot be built into one either, so a bilayer under a preparation that keeps them is refused at the box to change.

How the structure is oriented matters for a flow, because a folded or docked model is not oriented: its membrane normal is not z. Say its transmembrane segments as A:30-56, A:71-96, or orient the structure before the flow and leave the orientation as imported.

The scoring window, and where it runs

MM-GBSA averages over a slice of the production rather than all of it, and which slice is a real question: the first nanoseconds are the complex still settling into its box. Score from and Score to are that slice, and Score every is how often a frame of it is taken. It can be no finer than the frames kept, which is the other half of why the default frame count matters: at a frame every 200 ps nothing could ever be rescored more finely than 200 ps.

Residues held still in production holds named residues through the run, written the way the hotspots are, for a run meant to answer what the rest does while these stay put. Production restraint is how hard, and is the first rung of the equilibration ladder whether or not any residue is named.

GPU type is worth knowing about on Grace: the a40 nodes sit on a partition of their own and are often free while a100 jobs queue, and auto takes whichever type has a free card at submit. Run on can be CPU instead, which is one whole node for a short look while every card is busy: Amber or GROMACS, at most 10 ns and 500 frames, and the card's own bounds move to match the moment you choose it. CPU cores, Memory and Replicates running at once are the rest of the job: the last throttles a run's replicates rather than queueing all of them at once. A walltime typed into Walltime is each production job's limit, so a shorter one cuts a long run into more segments; left empty, a run past the queue's limit is chained into segments anyway.

The flow's own Results page then puts that card's runs side by side: the structure each one is of, its rank and the score it arrived with, anything it was told to differ in, and its binding energy and interface RMSD as its own analysis reports them.

Launching, and what it costs

Launch the flow asks the cluster what the flow would do and shows it: what each card will queue, where it lands, and the numbers that decide what it costs. Nothing is committed until Queue it. Once it is, the flow outlives the launcher, the laptop and the connection that queued it: each card is submitted with a dependency on the one before.

Watching one

Watch a queued flow opens it with how far it has got across the top: a bar that fills as stages finish, the share done, and every stage in the order they run, each marked done, queued, running, failed, stopped or waiting: queued while its job waits for a node, running once it has one. The stage at work pulses and its part of the bar moves. The page reads the record again every twenty seconds while anything is left to happen, and the flow carries on whether it is open or not.

Click any stage, in that row or on the drawing, and it opens beside the drawing: its job, its folder, how long it has been at it, the end of its log, and what it has made so far: structures to turn in a viewer, pictures, tables and reports, exactly as Results shows a run. The stage that needs you is the one shown first; while nothing does, the panel follows the stage at work, until you pick one yourself.

In the portal you will see two kinds of job: the short flow- ones, which are the engine's own bookkeeping and last seconds, and the real job named after the tool.

Its results

A flow's results are in as many folders as it has cards, so Results on a running or finished flow gathers them onto one page: the chain, what each card left, the first rows of its tables, and the files worth keeping. The page carries everything it shows, so it can be sent to somebody with no account on the cluster. Files on any card opens that card's folder.

Once nothing of a flow is left to happen, each docking run in it is tidied by its own tool, as the Tidy button on that tool's page does it: what the results no longer read is packed into one archive in the run's folder, and Undo tidy there puts it back. It waits for the flow to end, not for the docking, because a card after the docking may still be reading what it wrote. Keep every file of the run, on a docking card, leaves that run alone. The simulations tidy themselves when they finish, as they do when started from their own page.

When something fails

A stage that fails stops the stages after it, because each waits on the one before, unless a stage has another track still feeding it: HOPE-MD fed by both ADCP docking and HADDOCK docking runs on what the track that worked brought, and says so on its card. A stage that fails is tried once more by itself when the flow wakes it again, which covers a node that died under it; a second failure waits for you. A banner then says which stage, why, and which stages have finished and are kept, with what to do about it beside it. The end of the failed stage's own log is in the panel under it, so the reason is in front of you rather than in a file.

Carry on from the stage named on the button queues from there. What has already run is left alone: its folders are on disk, cost what they cost, and are not made again. That holds inside a simulation stage too: the runs it finished are kept, and only the ones that did not are queued again. A run's report or video failing does not fail the stage. This is the right answer when a flow stopped for a reason that has nothing to do with the flow: a node died, a queue was full, an allocation ran out overnight. A flow can also stall, with nothing of it left in the queue and nothing failed; the banner says so, the stage whose turn did not come is the one shown, with the log of the job that was to start it, and Carry on is the answer to that too.

A stage can fail while another track of the same flow is still running. The banner says so at once, but carrying on waits until nothing of the flow is left in the queue, so that nothing runs twice; until then it offers only Report this.

Fix and carry on is for when a setting was the problem. It puts the flow back on the canvas with the cards that have already run shown but not offered, so you can correct what has not run and carry on from there. Three things are refused, and each for the same reason: the record and the folders must not disagree. A flow with anything still in the queue has to be stopped or waited for first; a card that has finished cannot be changed, removed or rewired, because its results were made from what it was given; and a finished card cannot lose a link, because what the cards below it receive comes through it.

Report this, on a flow with a stage that failed or that has stalled, writes a report of what happened, with the jobs, that stage's own log and its settings already gathered. You see the whole report before it is sent.
