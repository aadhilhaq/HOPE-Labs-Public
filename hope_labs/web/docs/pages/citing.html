How to cite

HOPE Labs opens the tools; it does none of the science. Cite the tools whose results you report, and acknowledge the computing.

The tools

Each tool records in its runs what it used, and each tool's own documentation says how to cite it. Cite from there.

ToolBy
RFdiffusionthe Institute for Protein Design, University of Washington: github.com/RosettaCommons/RFdiffusion. The sequences are ProteinMPNN's, by its own authors
BoltzGenHannes Stärk and colleagues: github.com/HannesStark/boltzgen. It ranks by re-folding with Boltz-2, so cite that too
Boltz-2Jeremy Wohlwend, Gabriele Corso, Saro Passaro and colleagues, under the MIT license: github.com/jwohlwend/boltz. The alignments come from the ColabFold MSA server
ColabFoldMilot Mirdita, Konstantin Schütze, Yoshitaka Moriwaki, Lim Heo, Sergey Ovchinnikov and Martin Steinegger, under the MIT license: github.com/sokrypton/ColabFold. The folding is AlphaFold 2 and AlphaFold-Multimer, by DeepMind, whose weights are under CC BY 4.0
HOPE-MDthe HOPE Lab

The methods the tools run, AlphaFold, ProteinMPNN, Boltz-2, Amber, OpenMM, GROMACS, NAMD, Desmond and the rest, belong to their authors.

Some of the tools are opened by a page of ours. RFdiffusion, RFdiffusion3, BoltzGen, Boltz-2 and ColabFold ship no page, so the lab wrote one, and each of those pages carries its own How to cite naming the version the install actually runs. Cite the tool and the methods it runs; the page is no more citable than this launcher is. ColabFold writes the citations for the exact configuration it used into every run, as cite.bibtex, which is the one to copy.

The viewer

A structure opened under Results, or from a flow's card, turns in 3Dmol.js, and its Surface is computed by MSMS on the cluster. A figure made in the viewer cites 3Dmol.js, and one that shows a surface cites MSMS as well:

CiteWhen
Rego N, Koes D. 3Dmol.js: molecular visualization with WebGL. Bioinformatics 31(8):1322 to 1324, 2015. doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btu829 a figure from the viewer
Sanner MF, Olson AJ, Spehner JC. Reduced surface: an efficient way to compute molecular surfaces. Biopolymers 38(3):305 to 320, 1996. doi:10.1002/(SICI)1097-0282(199603)38:3<305::AID-BIP4>3.0.CO;2-Y a figure with a surface from the viewer

MSMS is Michel Sanner's, and it is run through mslib, the MSMS library in the ADCP 1.1 suite, where a cluster has that suite. THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md gives its terms.

Terms

HOPE Labs is the lab's, all rights reserved: LICENSE at the root of the repository is the statement, and the bar along the bottom of every page reads its copyright line out of that file rather than repeating it by hand. The libraries it is built from, and the tools it starts, keep their own licenses. THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md beside it lists them, separating what travels inside the download from what is only an install on the cluster, and says where each tool's own notices are. Both files travel in the download.

A tool's own license reaches the launcher. HOPE Labs signs in as you and starts the copy installed on your cluster, so a page it serves is that copy's page. Where a tool you install forbids offering it to third parties as a hosted service, do not offer a page it serves to other people. Each tool's own How to cite page says what its terms are.

The computing

Everything here runs on Texas A&M's clusters. HOPE Labs prints this sentence in its log each time you sign in, for the acknowledgments of anything that used them:

Please acknowledge Texas A&M High Performance Research Computing (HPRC) for the computational resources.
