RFdiffusion on Grace

A page in front of the lab's shared RFdiffusion install: give it a target, say where the binder should bind, and it queues one GPU job that diffuses binder backbones onto the target and gives each of them a sequence.

What the page does

RFdiffusion designs a protein backbone. Given a receptor and the residues a binder should touch, it diffuses a new chain of the length you ask for onto that patch of the surface, keeping the receptor exactly as it was. A backbone has no sequence, so the same job then runs ProteinMPNN over every design, with the target held fixed, and keeps the best sequence it proposes for the binder. What comes out is a structure of the binder already placed on the target, and a sequence for it.

This page takes the target, the hotspots and the binder length, checks them against the structure while you type, shows the contig map and the hotspot list the job will be given, writes the run folder and queues the job. Nothing is installed and no module is loaded: the code, the environment and the model weights are all in the install at ML_programs/RFdiffusion in the HOPE Labs folder, and ProteinMPNN is unpacked beside it.

The server runs as you. The job is queued under your own account, Slurm charges your allocation, and the page shows only the runs you can read.

The two screens

ScreenWhat it is for
DesignThe target, the hotspots, the binder length and the resources, with a box that says what Slurm will be asked for and another that shows the contig map before anything is queued. Submit stays off while anything is unanswered.
RunsYour runs and their Slurm state, the backbones and sequences written so far, the designs best first once the run has finished, the log, and where the results are. A job can be canceled here, and one that stopped can be queued again.

The same run, from a flow

This tool can also be one card of a flow, drawn on the launcher's canvas with a simulation after it. The page and the canvas queue the run through the same code, so the two are the same run: the same job script, the same plan, the same run folder, and the same refusals for the same reasons. The only difference is that nobody had to draw a graph to do one thing.

A run queued from either place can be read on the Runs screen here, and a design it leaves can be handed to HOPE-MD from a flow.

What it is not

A design from this run has not been validated. RFdiffusion proposes a backbone and ProteinMPNN proposes a sequence for it. Neither of them predicts the complex again to see whether the design would actually fold and bind that way. Folding the complex again, with Boltz-2 or ColabFold, is how that is checked. The score beside each design is ProteinMPNN's own, and it says how confident ProteinMPNN was about the sequence, not how well the binder binds.

So a run here is the beginning of a design campaign rather than the end of one. The usual next step is to fold the complex and look at it, or to simulate it in HOPE-MD, before anything reaches a bench.

RFdiffusion is not the lab's work. It is by the Institute for Protein Design at the University of Washington, and ProteinMPNN is by its own authors. The bar along the bottom of the page names the version this install runs, and How to cite says what to cite.
