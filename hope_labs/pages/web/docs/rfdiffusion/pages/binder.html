The binder

How long the binder should be, how many to make, the knobs that change what the diffusion does rather than how it is run, and the sequence step that follows it.

The length range

Shortest and Longest are a range, and a length is sampled inside it for every backbone. The default is 70 to 100 residues, which is a small folded miniprotein: long enough to have a hydrophobic core and a face to bind with, short enough to be synthesised and expressed.

A wide range gives designs that are hard to compare with each other, and the page says so when the range is wider than about sixty residues. If you want one length, set both boxes to it.

Below about thirty residues a designed chain will not fold on its own. It can still be a useful peptide, and the docking downstream takes nothing longer than thirty, but it is a different kind of design from a miniprotein and RFdiffusion was not trained for it.

Backbones and sequences

Backbones to generate is the whole size of the run. Each one is a separate diffusion from a different starting noise, so ten backbones are ten independent attempts at the same site, not ten views of one answer. Minutes each, and the run is this many.

Sequences per backbone is how many sequences ProteinMPNN proposes for each design. It runs with the target held fixed and only the diffused chain free, so what it designs is the binder and nothing else. The best of them, by ProteinMPNN's own score, is the one kept and written into designs.json; the rest stay in the run folder under proteinmpnn/ if you want to look at them.

Eight is the default and is usually plenty. The two numbers multiply, so a hundred backbones with eight sequences each is eight hundred sequence designs, and the page warns when the product gets large enough to matter to the walltime.

The diffusion's own knobs

These sit behind More settings, because the defaults are the ones RFdiffusion's own binder-design example uses and most runs should leave them alone.

SettingWhat it does
Noise scaleHow much noise the denoiser is given, on both the positions and the frames. The example lowers it to 0 for binder design, which is what makes the backbones worth giving a sequence to. Raising it asks for more varied designs, and fewer of them will fold.
Diffusion stepsHow many denoising steps each design takes. Fifty is the default. Fewer is faster and rougher, and fifteen is the least the box takes: RFdiffusion asserts on anything lower while it builds its schedule, so a smaller number spends a card and writes nothing.
Noise scheduleWhether the noise scale above holds for the whole run (constant, RFdiffusion's own) or walks from it to the figure below over the steps (linear).
Noise scale at the last stepWhere a linear schedule ends up. It is read only on that schedule: a constant one returns the starting scale at every step and never looks at this, and the box says so when the schedule is constant.
CheckpointWhich model weights to use. Empty takes the binder-design default, which is the right answer unless you have a reason. The two on the menu are the complex checkpoints; see below for why the rest of the install's weights are not offered.
Keep the diffusion trajectoriesOff. RFdiffusion's own default writes two files per design holding every step of it, which nothing downstream reads. Turn it on to look at the diffusion itself, and expect thousands of files.
Repeat the same designsSeeds each design from its own number, so the same card run twice gives the same backbones. There is no seed box: this is the whole of RFdiffusion's reproducibility, and the number it uses is the design's index.

Pulling on the design

Pull on the design adds one of RFdiffusion's potentials: a force applied to the binder at every step, beside the model's own prediction. Empty is the default and is what the binder-design example uses.

PullWhat it asks for
binder_ROGA compact binder, by its radius of gyration. The one to reach for when designs come back stringy.
binder_ncontactsMore contacts inside the binder, which is the same wish from the other side.
interface_ncontactsMore contacts between the binder and the target: a larger interface rather than a better binder.

Three of RFdiffusion's eight potentials, and they are the three written for a binder beside a target. The others describe a monomer or a symmetric oligomer and have no binder to act on in a run shaped like this one, so they are not offered and the job refuses them by name.

Start without one. RFdiffusion's own notes say the potentials "seem to interact weirdly with hotspot residues" in binder design, that its authors know why and mean to write better ones for it, and that good practice is to begin with none and raise the strength slowly. This card is always aimed at hotspots, so that is advice about this exact run. A baseline with no pull at all is the thing to compare against.

How hard it pulls is the potential's own weight, 1 by default. Guide scale is how much of the pull reaches the diffusion, 10 by default, and Guide decay is how it fades as the structure settles: constant, linear, quadratic or cubic. All three are read only once a pull is chosen, and say so until it is.

The sequence step

ProteinMPNN is half of what this card does: its temperature, its seed and its model are boxes under More settings, each at ProteinMPNN's own default.

SettingWhat it does
ProteinMPNN modelv_48_020 by default. The figure is the backbone noise the model was trained with, in hundredths of an angstrom: v_48_002 stays closest to the backbone it is given and v_48_030 is the most forgiving of a rough one.
Soluble ProteinMPNNWeights trained on soluble proteins only, which is what a binder has to be. The install carries both sets, so this is a choice rather than a download.
Sequence temperature0.1, which is both ProteinMPNN's default and what this job always used. Higher is more varied; ProteinMPNN suggests 0.1 to 0.3.
Sequence seed37, which is the number that was frozen in, so a card drawn before any of this designs the sequences it always did. 0 picks a new one each run.
Amino acids to leave outLetters the designed sequence may not use. C keeps cysteines out, which is usual for something to be expressed. X is always among them whatever you type.
Backbone noise0 by default. Noise added to the backbone while the sequence is designed, up to the 0.30 A the released models were trained at.

What the form does not offer

Relaxing a design before it is scored. It was on this card and it did nothing: relaxing needs the interface script from dl_binder_design, and that needs a PyRosetta licence this install has not got. It has been taken off rather than hidden, because a control that changes nothing is worse than no control. What the run does instead is call ProteinMPNN's own script directly on the diffused backbone, which needs no PyRosetta and is what has been proven here end to end.

Six of the nine checkpoint files. The install holds nine, and eight of those are diffusion checkpoints. RFdiffusion picks the complex model by itself once hotspots are given, and that is what this card always asks for; the others are for jobs this card does not do, which is fold-guided scaffolding, sequence inpainting and unconditional or active-site design. The ninth file is the structure prediction weights and is not a diffusion checkpoint at all.

A contig of your own. The contig is built from the target: every resolved stretch of every chain kept, a chain break, then the binder's length range. A fixed motif, several designed segments or inpainting would each need a contig written by hand, and the job's own way of telling the binder from the target afterwards is built on there being exactly one designed chain of the length that was asked for. That is a change to the job, not a box on the form.

Partial diffusion, symmetry, and ProteinMPNN's per-position files. Partial diffusion re-noises a design that already exists, and what this card is given is a target with no binder on it. Symmetry is for a symmetric assembly rather than a binder against one face. ProteinMPNN's fixed-position, bias and tied-position options each want a file of per-position dictionaries, which is not something a one-line box on a card can carry.
