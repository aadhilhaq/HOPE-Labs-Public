What a run leaves

One folder per run, holding the structure that went in, the job that was sent, the backbones, the sequences, and one small file that the rest of the lab's tools read.

The run folder

pdl1_20261003-1204/              the run, as the Runs screen lists it
  flow.json                      what was asked for, as a flow of two cards
  submission.json                who submitted it, when, the job id, the plan
  inputs/target.pdb              the structure, fetched or copied once
  state/rfdiffusion.json         what became of the card
  pdl1_rfdiffusion/              the job's own folder
    plan.json                    what the job was told
    job.sbatch                   the script that was queued
    slurm_<jobid>.out            the log
    rfdiffusion/                 the backbones, design_0.pdb onwards
    proteinmpnn/                 one folder per backbone, with every sequence
    designs.json                 the designs, best first
    cache/                       what the job wrote for itself

The two levels are not an accident. The outer folder is a flow of two cards, a target and the tool on it, which is exactly what the page submits and exactly what the canvas would have drawn; the inner one is the job's own. That is why a run queued from the canvas and a run queued here look the same on disk and can both be read here.

designs.json

The one file anything else reads. It is written when the run finishes, and it holds the designs best first:

{"tool": "rfdiffusion",
 "designs": [{"name": "design_3",
              "path": ".../rfdiffusion/design_3.pdb",
              "binder_chains": ["A"],
              "target_chains": ["B"],
              "sequence": "SPEELKAIAEE...",
              "score": 1.284}]}

Which chain is the binder and which is the target is read out of each design rather than assumed. RFdiffusion writes the diffused chain first and the target after it, which is the opposite way round from the contig that asked for them, so the chains are matched against the sequences of the target that went in: whatever is left over, and is the length that was asked for, is the binder. A design where that cannot be settled is skipped and named in the log rather than passed on the wrong way round.

The backbones and the sequences

rfdiffusion/design_0.pdb and its siblings are the raw diffusion output: the target kept verbatim and the binder as a chain of glycines, because a backbone has no sequence yet. They are the structures designs.json points at, and the sequence in the record is the one to read onto them.

proteinmpnn/design_0/seqs/design_0.fa holds every sequence ProteinMPNN proposed for that backbone, with its score in each header, and the backbone's own glycine sequence as the first record. The page keeps the best; the file is there if you want the others.

What the score is, and is not

The score is ProteinMPNN's, and it is about the sequence. It is the model's negative log likelihood for the sequence it designed, so lower is better and a design at 0.9 is one ProteinMPNN was more sure about than one at 1.4. It says nothing about whether the binder binds, how tightly, or whether it folds at all.

Nothing in this run predicts the complex again. That is the gap between a run here and a BindCraft2 campaign, which spends most of its time refolding candidates and throwing away the ones that do not hold up. Treat the designs as proposals to be filtered, not as results.

Taking a design further

From the launcher's canvas, the designed complexes output of this card goes straight into HOPE-MD, which will simulate the binder on the target and compute a binding energy for it. The design sequences output goes into the docking instead, for designs short enough for it to take, which is thirty residues or fewer.

By hand: the structure named in each record is a complete complex and can be opened, folded again, or handed to anything that reads a PDB.
