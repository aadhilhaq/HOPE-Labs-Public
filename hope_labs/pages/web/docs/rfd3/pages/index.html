RFdiffusion3 on Grace

Designs a binder onto a target by diffusing every atom at once, rather than a frame per residue. This page queues that run on a GPU node and gives each design a sequence.

What it is

RFdiffusion3 is a generative model for biomolecules from the Institute for Protein Design. The difference from RFdiffusion, and the reason it is a separate tool here rather than an upgrade, is the unit it works in: RFdiffusion diffuses a frame per residue and places side chains afterwards, and RFdiffusion3 diffuses the atoms themselves. A design can therefore be asked for by the atoms that have to be in the right place, which is what an enzyme active site or a metal site is.

For the binder design this page offers, that difference matters less than it does for an active site. It is still the newer model, and its authors report it beating RFdiffusion on every one of their five binder benchmarks.

It stands beside RFdiffusion, not over it

Both tools are on the Tools page and both have a card on the canvas. They cannot share an environment: RFdiffusion runs on Python 3.9 in SE3nv and this needs 3.12, so they are two installs. Nothing has been retired, and a flow saved against RFdiffusion still runs.

What they hand on is identical, so anything that could follow one can follow the other: a complex with the binder already placed on the target, and the binder's sequence.

What a run does

  1. Builds the input the model is driven by: a contig naming the binder's length and the target's residues, and the hotspots the binder has to reach.
  2. Diffuses the designs, all of them in one pass on the GPU.
  3. Gives each binder a sequence with ProteinMPNN, and keeps the model's own sequence beside it.
  4. Writes designs.json, which is what the next card reads.
Two designs against MDM2, sequences included, took eleven minutes on one A100. The model's own demo, twenty-four designs across three harder problems, took twenty-four.
