The target and hotspots

What the binder is designed against, which face of it, and the one thing about the numbering that will catch you out.

The target

A PDB id to fetch, a file to drop on the page, or a path to a file already on Grace. Both .pdb and .cif are read: an mmCIF is written as a PDB first, which is the file the run is given, and the contig you are shown is read off that copy. Chains names the chains the binder is designed against; everything else in the file is ignored rather than kept out of the way, so a structure that arrived with its own bound peptide should have that chain left out.

Hotspots

The residues the binder has to reach, written as A:63,A:67,A:73. They do two things. They are passed to the model as the residues the design must contact, and they decide where the design is grown from: the origin is placed ten angstroms out from their center of mass, so the binder forms off the face you named rather than wherever the target's center happens to be.

The model takes hotspots at the atom level as well, naming particular side-chain atoms. This page sends whole residues, because that is what a target card carries. For binder design that is the normal case; the atom-level form is what the active-site work in the paper uses.

No hotspots is allowed and is not the same as a mistake. With none named, the origin falls back to the target's center of mass and the binder may grow anywhere on it. The run says so in its log rather than refusing.

Pressing the site onto the chain

Under the target the page draws each chain you are keeping in the structure's own numbering, ten residues to a group with the number above each, every residue a button. The box and the chain are one site, so typing in one marks the other. A number typed into a box cannot be checked by eye against the structure it names, and that is the mistake on this page that never announces itself: a site aimed at the wrong face designs for twelve hours and fails nothing.

A residue drawn without a letter is one this page's own reader has no residue for; the number is still the one a hotspot is written in. Where a structure uses insertion codes, so that 21, 21A and 21B are three residues and one number, every residue is drawn and only the first of each number can be pressed: a hotspot is a number and has nowhere to put the code.

Taking the site from a bound partner

Most targets worth designing against arrive bound to something. 1YCR is MDM2 with the p53 peptide in its cleft, and the residues that peptide touches are the site a binder wants. Leave the partner's chain out of the target and the page offers Site from chain B, which marks every kept residue within 4.5 Å of it and says how many it marked and from where. On 1YCR that is 22 residues of chain A. Prune them if you want a tighter site: a design asked to reach 22 residues at once is being asked for a larger binder than one aimed at seven.

4.5 Å is the distance every page here counts a contact at, so a site taken from a partner and an interface read off a complex are the same residues by the same rule. The partner need not be protein and need not be a chain this tool's own reader can see: a DNA strand, a ligand or a sugar in a chain of its own is a partner, because a contact is measured on atoms. The page names any such chain under the target.

Site from all chains asks the other question, the one a structure of three chains kept whole asks: not what the chain you left out touches but where these chains touch each other at all. It marks every residue of the chains you keep within 4.5 Å of any other chain in the file, the ones you are designing against included. On a trimer kept whole it is the only reading there is.

The numbering, which is not yours

Two renumberings happen, and only the first of them is visible on the form.

Going in, the contig has to name the target's residues as a range, and that range is read off the structure rather than assumed. A cropped PDB keeps its original numbering: MDM2 in 1YCR runs from 25 to 109, not from 1, and the contig says A25-109. Assuming a range starting at 1 would be perfectly well formed and would design against the wrong residues.

Show the job, under the run plan beside Submit, shows that contig, with the hotspots and the origin strategy beside it, as the job will be given them:

contig: 70-100,/0,A25-109
select_hotspots: A63, A67, A73, each as a whole residue ("ALL")
infer_ori_strategy: hotspots, so the binder grows off the face they are on

Read it before you submit: the range it took off your structure, and every residue it will aim at, are both in there.

Coming out, the model relabels and renumbers everything it writes, in the order the contig put it. The design comes back as chain A numbered from 1, and the target follows as chain B renumbered from 1, whatever it was called and however it was numbered on the way in. What a run leaves says what that means for reading the output.

A target with gaps in its chain

Most crystal structures have holes in them. Residues nobody could see in the density are not in the file at all, and the numbering steps over them: 7A3R's chain A is numbered 1 to 395 but has no 191 to 194, no 226 or 227, and three more holes besides. The target is given to the tool as it was deposited, so the holes come with it.

The contig names each stretch of resolved residues on its own, with a chain break between them, so 7A3R's chain A goes in as six pieces:

70-100,/0,A1-190,/0,A195-225,/0,A228-270,/0,A275-342,/0,A345-381,/0,A386-395

A break is what a hole in the chain really is, because there is no peptide bond across it. Written without one, the stretches either side would be bonded together and renumbered as though the backbone ran straight through, which is a claim about the protein that is not true. The target therefore comes back under one chain letter per stretch, B to G for this one, and the design is still chain A.

A hotspot that falls in a hole is refused by name before anything is queued, because there is no such residue to aim at. The chips above the box say how each chain is numbered but not where its holes are, so a refusal naming the residue is how you find out.
