The target and hotspots

What the binder is designed against, which face of it, and the one thing about the numbering that will catch you out.

The target

A PDB id to fetch, or a path to a file already on Grace. Both .pdb and .cif are read. Chains names the chains the binder is designed against; everything else in the file is ignored rather than kept out of the way, so a structure that arrived with its own bound peptide should have that chain left out.

Hotspots

The residues the binder has to reach, written as A:63,A:67,A:73. They do two things. They are passed to the model as the residues the design must contact, and they decide where the design is grown from: the origin is placed ten angstroms out from their centre of mass, so the binder forms off the face you named rather than wherever the target's centre happens to be.

The model takes hotspots at the atom level as well, naming particular side-chain atoms. This page sends whole residues, because that is what a target card carries. For binder design that is the normal case; the atom-level form is what the active-site work in the paper uses.

No hotspots is allowed and is not the same as a mistake. With none named, the origin falls back to the target's centre of mass and the binder may grow anywhere on it. The run says so in its log rather than refusing.

The numbering, which is not yours

Two renumberings happen, and only the first of them is visible on the form.

Going in, the contig has to name the target's residues as a range, and that range is read off the structure rather than assumed. A cropped PDB keeps its original numbering: MDM2 in 1YCR runs from 25 to 109, not from 1, and the contig says A25-109. Assuming a range starting at 1 would be perfectly well formed and would design against the wrong residues.

The box under the hotspots shows that contig, with the hotspots and the origin strategy beside it, as the job will be given them:

contig: 70-100,/0,A25-109
select_hotspots: A63, A67, A73, each as a whole residue ("ALL")
infer_ori_strategy: hotspots, so the binder grows off the face they are on

Read it before you submit: the range it took off your structure, and every residue it will aim at, are both in there.

Coming out, the model relabels and renumbers everything it writes, in the order the contig put it. The design comes back as chain A numbered from 1, and the target follows as chain B renumbered from 1, whatever it was called and however it was numbered on the way in. What a run leaves says what that means for reading the output.

A target with gaps in its chain

Most crystal structures have holes in them. Residues nobody could see in the density are not in the file at all, and the numbering steps over them: 7A3R's chain A is numbered 1 to 395 but has no 191 to 194, no 226 or 227, and three more holes besides. The target is given to the tool as it was deposited, so the holes come with it.

The contig names each stretch of resolved residues on its own, with a chain break between them, so 7A3R's chain A goes in as six pieces:

70-100,/0,A1-190,/0,A195-225,/0,A228-270,/0,A275-342,/0,A345-381,/0,A386-395

A break is what a hole in the chain really is, because there is no peptide bond across it. Written without one, the stretches either side would be bonded together and renumbered as though the backbone ran straight through, which is a claim about the protein that is not true. The target therefore comes back under one chain letter per stretch, B to G for this one, and the design is still chain A.

A hotspot that falls in a hole is refused by name before anything is queued, because there is no such residue to aim at. The chips above the box say how each chain is numbered but not where its holes are, so a refusal naming the residue is how you find out.
