The binder

What kind of binder to design, how long it should be, how many to make, and the three settings that change how the model generates them.

What to design

What to design is BoltzGen's protocol, and it is the whole of what the model is being asked. It is the first thing to choose and the one setting a run cannot be queued without a decision on.

ProtocolWhat it designs
protein-anythingA protein binder against a target of any kind. The default, and the right answer for a protein receptor.
peptide-anythingA peptide binder. Use a short length range with this, and see Cyclic peptide below.
nanobody-anythingA nanobody, with the framework the model knows and the loops designed.
antibody-anythingAn antibody, in the same way.
protein-small_moleculeA protein binder against a small molecule target rather than a protein one.
protein-redesignRedesign of a binder that is already there, rather than a new one.

A peptide and a nanobody against the same target are two different runs. There is no setting that turns one into the other, which is why this is a protocol and not a tick box.

The length range

Shortest and Longest are the binder's own length, and they go into the specification as a range the model samples inside, written 70..100.

This is the binder's length, not the complex's. BoltzGen also has a setting for the length of the whole complex, target included, which it satisfies by resampling until something fits. That is not what this form sets, and it is deliberately left out: a number that includes the target is a number nobody can reason about, and the run leaves it to the model.

The default 70 to 100 residues is a small folded miniprotein. For a peptide protocol, something like 12 to 20 is more appropriate.

How many designs

Designs to generate is the size of the run. Each design is generated, inverse folded, re-folded with the target and scored, so the cost per design is higher here than in a tool that only generates. Ten is the default.

The ranking at the end comes from an analysis step over all of them, and that step is the part that has been reported running out of card memory at a hundred designs. Several runs of twenty are safer than one of a hundred, and they can be read together.

The model's own settings

These sit behind More settings, because the protocol's own defaults are the considered answer for almost every run.

SettingWhat it does
Cyclic peptideAsks for a cyclic binder, by adding one line to the specification. It applies to the peptide protocols; on a protein protocol it is ignored by the model rather than refused.
Step scaleBoltzGen's --step_scale. It scales the diffusion step, and despite how it reads it is not a count of steps. 0 leaves the protocol's own value alone, which is what almost every run should do.
Fold and score each designOn by default, and the ranking comes from it: the designs are re-folded with the target and scored, and that score is the order they come out in. Turning it off stops the run after generation and inverse folding, which is faster and leaves the designs unranked and designs.json in no meaningful order.
Turning off folding turns off the only filter in the run. Without it, what you get is whatever the model generated, with no check that it folds that way. It is useful for seeing quickly whether a target and a protocol produce anything at all, and it is not a shortcut to designs worth taking further.
